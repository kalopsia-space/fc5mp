## Partner-function probe

Probed over each function's full extent (primary entry + chained fragments):

| Function | `cmp reg, imm` (imm 1–8) | indexed array access (`[base + idx*scale]`) |
|---|---|---|
| `0x18a896770` CreateCoopLeash | many × `cmp …, 1`, one × `cmp …, 3` | none |
| `0x18a169c30` DoSpawnPlayer | one × `cmp …, 4` (at `0x18a169c43`), then `cmp …, 1` ×3 | `r8, [rax + rcx*4]` (`0x18a169ef0`); `r9, [rax + rcx*4]` (`0x18a169f00`) |

Interpretation, held loosely but now pointing one way:
- **DoSpawnPlayer is the strongest lead toward feasibility.** It compares a value against **4** near the top of the function and then indexes a 4-byte-stride array by a *register* (`[rax + rcx*4]`) — the shape of a loop/lookup over an indexable per-player array bounded by a small constant, not two hardwired slots. If `rcx` is a player index and the `4` is its bound, co-op player storage may already be a widenable array. **Unconfirmed** — the register's meaning must be read by hand (next steps).
- The leash function compares mostly against **1**, consistent with "is the single partner present?" boolean logic rather than iterating a capped player array. Mild evidence that the *leash*, at least, is written around one partner.

## GATE DECISION: **INCONCLUSIVE**

Static analysis cannot yet answer whether co-op player storage is generic (GO) or fixed 2-wide (STOP). Two independent reasons:
1. **Coverage gaps in the toolkit** left 3 of 8 anchors (including a key leash-callback path) unresolved, so the picture is incomplete by construction.
2. **The signals obtained are thin and mixed** — a promising `cmp …, 4` in DoSpawnPlayer, boolean-looking leash logic, but no decisive read of the player container's width.

Forcing GO or STOP from this would be guessing. The feasibility question stays open, and the next steps are cheap and well-defined.

### Next steps (before the gate can close)
1. **Widen scanner coverage:** scan the secondary `.code` region for `lea` xrefs, and accept LEA-validated string copies regardless of NUL-prefix. Re-run; expect the 3 `no-xref` anchors to resolve.
2. **Manually disassemble the two resolved partner functions** — CreateCoopLeash `0x18a896770` and DoSpawnPlayer `0x18a169c30` — to read how the partner/player is stored and what the `cmp …, 4` gates. This is the single most informative next action.
3. **Find and read the "add player to session" path** (`CStormEchoAddSessionPlayerOp`, `CCoopServiceNetObjectP2P`) to see the container that holds remote players and whether its width is a constant 2.
4. **Resolve the player-capacity constants** (`nMaxNumPlayers`, `MaxPlayers`) to their defining structures.

## Confidence & caveats

- **Static only.** `FC_m64.dll` carries Denuvo markers; the on-disk bytes of protected regions may differ from what executes. Any eventual GO must be re-validated at runtime in P1 (passive capture + live inspection) before widening work begins.
- The toolkit's building blocks (VA mapping, function table with chain-info resolution, LEA scanner, anchor resolution, call graph, operand probe) are unit-tested against this exact DLL build and are reusable for steps 1–4 above and for P1.
